# Fruit Box solvers, competition, and complexity

Research checked 29 October 2026. This note distinguishes the original GameSaien-style random board, the 2022 programming challenge, the 2026 complexity talk, and this project's solvable-board generator. They share the rectangle-sums-to-ten move rule but ask different questions.

The challenge's “optimal” wording means the **objective** was to remove the most apples, not that submissions proved optimality. ## What “NP-hard” can and cannot mean here

Let a generalized board have variable dimensions, digits 1–9 in occupied cells, and empty cells treated as zero. A move selects an axis-aligned rectangle whose **currently occupied** digits sum to ten, then clears those digits. Three distinct problems are:

1. **Marked-apple reachability:** Is there a legal sequence that clears a designated apple? This is the variant named on the [video's decision-problem slide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1nTdHEQRmMM&t=140s). Its reduction slide starts from [SAT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1nTdHEQRmMM&t=243s), using designed gadget boards rather than a randomly generated 10×17 start.
2. **Score threshold:** Is there a legal sequence clearing at least *K* occupied cells? This is the decision version of score maximization. A [first-party analysis by OshiZi](https://oshizi.com/blog/solving-apple-game-optimally/) explicitly says that it does **not** supply an NP-hardness proof for this version.
3. **Full clear:** Can every occupied cell be removed? This is also different from making one marked cell removable; the video comments themselves raise it as a separate question. A total sum divisible by ten is necessary but does not establish reachability of a full clear.

I inspected the video's stated decision question and SAT/gadget slides. I did **not** find a written reduction with a formal gadget verification, and have not independently established that its construction excludes unintended rectangle moves. The defensible attribution is “the talk **claims/presents** NP-hardness for marked-apple reachability on generalized, constructed boards.” It does **not** prove that the contest's maximum-score objective is NP-hard, that arbitrary original 10×17 boards are NP-hard as an asymptotic family, or that the solvable generator's boards are hard to solve. Showing hardness for one decision variant does not automatically transfer to another; such a transfer needs its own reduction.

The score-threshold and marked-apple variants are at least in NP under these rules: a certificate contains at most *N* clearing moves for *N* initially occupied cells, and replaying and checking each rectangle takes polynomial time. Asymptotic NP-hardness concerns variable-size inputs, while the ordinary game fixes the board at 10×17. A fixed 10×17 board has a finite number of possible states, even though exact search may be practically expensive. There are `10·11/2 × 17·18/2 = 8,415` possible coordinate rectangles at any one state; a live/cleared mask has at most `2^170` possibilities for a fixed initial board. These counts explain why legal-move enumeration is manageable while exhaustive sequence search is not.

## Board generation changes the answer

The [2022 contest specification](https://codegolf.stackexchange.com/questions/240548/play-the-fruit-box-game) says its starting boards are randomly populated 1–9 digits with total sum divisible by ten. A separate [empirical study by AppleGame+](https://apple.oshizi.com/about/board-generation/) captured 500 boards from the original game and found data consistent with uniform independent digits conditioned on total sum divisible by ten. Rejection sampling is the study's **hypothesis**, not a published GameSaien source-code fact. Divisibility alone does not guarantee a full-clear sequence. The [xalbd bot](https://github.com/xalbd/fruitbox-solver) exploits lower board totals as a **heuristic** for easier/higher-scoring rounds.

By contrast, [weizixiao/fruit-box-game's algorithm document](https://github.com/weizixiao/fruit-box-game/blob/main/docs/ALGORITHM.md) says its generator creates a complete legal sequence and then lays the digit groups into the board. It returns `{board, solution}`; the solution steps cover each cell exactly once, and replaying them clears all 170. The repository [README](https://github.com/weizixiao/fruit-box-game) states the same guarantee. For a **fresh board from that generator with its witness available**, the maximum possible apple score is immediately certified as 170 (ignoring the clock and human/automation drag time). Finding a good sequence only becomes a separate search problem after the witness is hidden/unavailable, a player deviates so that the recorded next steps cease to be legal, or the task asks to optimize another objective such as number of drags or elapsed time. The generator's own documentation says hints use the next certified step when still valid, then fall back to a live move scan after deviations.

## Practical implications for solver work

- Fix the target first: maximum apples for an arbitrary board, fastest 170 clear for a certified board, best real-time mouse play, or marked-apple reachability are different tasks.
- For this project's generated fresh boards, replay and verify the witness before investing in combinatorial search. A legal 170-clear is a score-optimality certificate because no board has more than 170 apples.
- If solving arbitrary or deviated boards, keep move generation separate from move choice. Enumerate legal rectangles with row-pair accumulation or 2D prefix sums, represent the remaining board as a bitmask, and replay every proposed solution against the game rules. Search with a time budget can produce improving **lower bounds**; only exhaustive search with sound pruning and a matching upper bound proves an optimum.
- Do not call a current-board set-packing ILP or a width-limited DFS “optimal” for the sequential game. New legal rectangles can emerge after clearing, so a static list misses legal continuations.

## Source limitations

- The 2026 talk is a recorded presentation, and the inspected slide shows *marked-apple reachability*. No peer-reviewed or self-contained written proof was located in this search. The “NP-hard” statement is therefore attributed to the speaker, with proof validity unverified.
- The original GameSaien page returned HTTP 403 to this solver researcher's text crawler. A separate source inspection in this research session obtained and statically analyzed its delivered script; see [origins-and-generation.md](origins-and-generation.md) for the direct code evidence. The empirical study's rejection-sampling procedure is distributionally consistent under ideal randomness, but is not the exact implementation observed in GameSaien's current script.
- Public bot README claims were checked against source where a guarantee mattered. No code from these repositories was executed.
